# M8.9 "The look" — design and implementation plan

Executor: the agent that starts on Marcin's word. Reviewer: Claude, at the
gate. Director and the only tester: Marcin. This document is the milestone's
design and its contract in one place, as M8.8's: the game's look taken from
Marcin's six out of ten to nine or ten. The world at a real golden hour and
lit at dusk, one colour for one meaning, one typeface in two styles, a HUD
that scales with the screen, one message at a time, a radar that answers
three questions, signs that read from afar and never block the view, and the
garage as a showroom with a picture on every card. Written 2026-09-25
against commit `52db2bc` (0.8.7) from a review of every screen (§1.1) that
Marcin accepted whole, with his bar: "6/10: it must be 10/10, at least 9".
About nineteen days of slices, and about six more for the atmosphere (R13,
Phase G), which Marcin added on 2026-09-28: "the sun, the clouds, the
atmosphere at a really high level"; and about five and a half for the
camera (R14, Phase H), which he added the same day after the research on
the reverse view.

**Where it sits.** After M8.8's gate and before M9, by the number Marcin
gave it (2026-09-25). The look dresses what the fleet adds: every new body
has its picture in the showroom, the sea and the dirt take the new light, and
the platform's thumbnail, cover and screenshots (M9) are taken of this look.
**Slice 0 does not wait**: it fixes two faults in the build Marcin plays and
goes in on his word before M8.8's slice 0, or first thing if M8.8 has begun.

Read, in this order, before touching anything: `CLAUDE.md`, §1 of this file,
`docs/DESIGN.md` §17 (the clean screen), §19 (Polish) and §20 (the way),
`docs/STYLE.md` (UI, the run HUD, the job signs), `docs/M8.8_REPORT.md` (the
bodies, the ground and the sea it added), `docs/PROGRESS.md` (newest first),
then the rest of this file. Run `npm run verify`; green before the first
edit.

## 0. How to work on this milestone

- **Language, autonomy, scope, honesty**: as in `docs/history/M5_PLAN.md` §0.
- **Pace** (`CLAUDE.md`): a slice is the code, its pins, the quick verify,
  one commit, eight lines in PROGRESS. **One exception, because this
  milestone's subject is how the game looks**: a slice that changes what the
  screen or the world looks like ends with its stills: `npx playwright test
  e2e/look.spec.ts -g stills` (slice 1 adds it), the slice's own states at
  1280×720, under a minute, looked at before the commit; the numbers it
  prints go into the slice's PROGRESS lines. No other suite, no perf, no bot
  measurement per slice. Marcin approved the exception (2026-09-25).
- **Marcin's notes come first**, each a slice ahead of the queue.
- **Every slice ends in a build he can play**; the old look is not kept
  behind a switch (the before set of slice 1 is the comparison).
- **Plain words in every message to him**: the stars, the money, the map,
  the garage; never token, zoom, glow attribute or atlas.
- **Every new word has its Polish in the same slice** (`src/ui/pl.ts`,
  `tests/ui/lang.test.ts`) and its place in `tests/ui/words.test.ts`.
- **The phases run in order** (A to I), the slices inside a phase too.

## 1. The design

### 1.1 What there is (measured 2026-09-25)

Every state was captured in Polish at 800×450, 1280×720 and 1920×1080: the
intro step by step, every job kind's sign, card and line, a chase at three
and at five stars, busted, the door's four pages and its offer, the full map,
the pause, the four districts (about 170 frames; the review's sheet of twelve
is in PROGRESS). What holds: the calm drive shows §17's six; the type is one
consistent arcade voice (heavy italic capitals, flat black shadows); the
route on the radar and the signs' pictograms work; nothing is ugly. What
does not, measured:

1. **The world is grey, not candy.** On the four districts' calm frames the
   mean saturation is 0.18–0.21 and only 1.7–5.5 % of the pixels are over
   0.5, most of them the player's car. The sun is 1.8 at 51° over the
   horizon (`SUN_OFFSET`), a midday angle; the hemisphere's fill is 1.35,
   three quarters of the sun, and the shadows are at 0.72: sunlit and shaded
   faces differ little and nothing looks like the last hour of the evening.
   The facades' colours (`CITY_COLORS`) sit at saturation 0.1–0.35; no
   window or lamp is lit; the sky is two colours and has no sun.
2. **The HUD's words sit on the sky and only their shadow carries them.**
   Against the sky at the top of the frame ink reaches 1.8–2.3:1, yellow
   1.4–1.8, red 1.4–1.7 and cyan 1.1–1.4 (large text wants 3:1, small text
   4.5:1); on the asphalt 3.3–10:1. LOSE THEM (ZGUB ICH) in red on the pink
   sky is the weakest line of the chase.
3. **A colour means many things.** Yellow is money, and also the selected
   tab and button, the titles (PAUZA), the highway and the player's arrow
   and the caches on the radar, the combo's ×, a full boost bar and the
   wall's sentence. Cyan is the way, and also NEW BEST, the next car's line,
   the streak, the hunts, the best run, the escape bar, the video icon, the
   combo at ×5, a kept car's frame and the next rival's chip. Red is trouble
   and the lead of a teaching line (ZLECENIE · ZWOLNIJ W KÓŁKU).
4. **The type is the system's and the sizes are fixed.** Segoe UI on
   Windows, another face elsewhere. Heavy italic plus a −8° to −14° skew
   slants the letters about 20°; with a hard shadow at 12–14 px (the cards'
   lines, the hints, the labels) the Polish marks read badly. Words are
   11–20 px whatever the screen (NITRO 11, the hints 12, the district 12,
   the line 20); only the speed and the stars follow the height a little. At
   1920×1080 the goal line is 20 px; at 800×450 the radar (a 150 px floor)
   is a third of the height and the eight key hints wrap to five rows.
5. **The moments are crowded.** The intro's first second shows nine things
   and the stars' news. The busted moment in a session's first 12 s shows
   the line, two rows of hints, the news, the stars, the bag, BORROW with its
   disguise clock, the ticket and the officer: two opposite orders. The
   first job brings its card, then the chain's step card saying NEXT: BANK
   THE BAG while the delivery runs, then NEW: STREET RACE, then NEW: TIME
   TRIAL, about nine seconds of reading over a timed job.
6. **The radar carries sixteen kinds of mark in 165 px**: the route, the
   goal's badge, open and closed badges (14.5 px; their pictograms read as
   white dots), three garages and four landmarks on the rim (the tower is a
   cream triangle beside the player's yellow one), up to thirty caches as
   yellow dots, the units lit and on the beat, the search, a race's rivals,
   the player, N. The brightest lines are the white streets and the yellow
   highway, not the route. The full map's key names all seven job kinds
   before they are out, and the districts' names lie over its icons.
7. **The world's guidance.** A sign reads to about 50 m and is gone by 90;
   the ring on the asphalt is not seen; seven coins round every ring pull a
   player through it fast, and the ring says slow down. Two faults of M8.7:
   the pay over the nearest sign never leaves the screen's top-left corner
   (`PayLabel.show` compares the new point with `NaN` and never writes it),
   and driving through a ring's centre the camera flies through its sign,
   whose face fills the screen.
8. **What a child cannot read.** The biggest number on screen, the bag (44
   px yellow), has no glyph and no word; §17.4's sack was never drawn. Two ×
   show at once (the combo's ×1 top left, the bag's ×1,65 top right). The
   stars have three states (yellow, red and pulsing, pulsing over a cyan
   bar) and a red +n under them. The loop (crimes fill the bag, the stars
   multiply it, a garage banks it) is said only on the wall, after the first
   door. Three pictograms need their word: the police light (escape), the
   crash star (takedown rage), the hammer (mayhem).
9. **The garage is a text sheet over the car it dresses.** The cars, the
   kit and the rivals are colour swatches with names; the wall covers the
   car; the room is a grey box with a black ceiling seen from its back
   corner.
10. **Faults that are not faults.** At five stars the helicopter's cone and
    spot wash the road and the sky in a flat pale shape; a thinned car's
    4×4 screen door reads as a coarse halftone at the frame's bottom; the
    pause's veil lies under the goal line and the money.
11. **Words.** KÓŁKO and KRĄG for a ring; R is RESET in the hints and OD NOWA
    on WRAK; the busted card says W DOWOLNY KLAWISZ; two lines are in lower
    case; OSŁONA is an abstract word for a roofed street.

### 1.3 The rules (accepted by Marcin; the numbers set here)

**R1. One colour, one meaning.** On the screen, the maps and the world's
marks (the signs, the rings, the route):

| Meaning | Token | Colour | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| money: the bag, a pay, a price, a coin, a cache | `--money` | `#ffd23f` | the bag and its ×, the pays, the prices, the money pops, the caches |
| the way: where to go and what to press next | `--way` | `#2bd1ff` | the route, the goal's ring, sign and badge, the swap prompt |
| trouble: bad for you | `--trouble` | `#ff3b5c` | the stars while seen (with blue), WPADKA, WRAK, a clock under 10 s, a price out of reach, LOSE THEM, a race's rivals on the maps |
| the police | `--police` | `#3b82ff` | the units, the search, the helicopter, the stars' flash |
| everything else | `--ink` | `#f7f3ea` | words, the player on the maps, selection (a white frame or fill), the combo, titles that are not money |
| closed, off | `--off` | `#8d8a96` | closed rings, owned and not chosen, units on the beat, dimmed buttons |
| outline and panel | `--outline`, `--panel` | `#160e28`, 78 % | every outline, every panel |

The old uses move by the table: selection and titles to ink, the highway to
a wide pale road, the player's arrow to ink, the combo to ink, a full boost
to ink with one pulse, the wall's highlights (NEW BEST, the next car, the
streak, the hunts, the best run) to ink with their amounts in yellow, the
escape bar to ink, the video icon to an ink frame, a kept car's and the next
rival's frames to white, a teaching line's lead to ink. **The stars** are
ink when filled and flash red and blue at 2 Hz, like a light bar, while the
police see the player (steady red under `prefers-reduced-motion`); the
search's bar is ink. The world's own colours (paint, facades, billboards,
the sea) are not signals and stay out of the table.

**R2. One typeface, two styles.** Rubik (SIL Open Font License), in the
build as woff2 subsets of the game's characters: Bold 700, Black 900, Black
Italic 900, about 60 KB, preloaded, `font-display: swap`. STYLE's "no web
fonts" goes: its reasons (bytes, offline, licence) are met by 60 KB of OFL
files inside the build.

- **Display** (numbers, titles, the line's kind, the pays, a card's title;
  20 px and up): Black Italic, capitals, a 2 px outline and a 2×3 px flat
  shadow. **No text is skewed**: the italic carries the motion; panels and
  bars keep their −8°.
- **Label** (everything 18 px and under: hints, a card's line, the wall's
  lines, legends, names): Bold, upright, capitals, tracking 0.06 em, a
  1.5 px outline.
- Every text over the world has its outline. Numbers in tabular figures.
  The ★ in a text is the HUD's star drawn inline, not a font's glyph.

**R3. The HUD scales with the screen.** Its sizes are in rem, and the root's
font size is 16 px × the scale: the window's height over 720, held between
0.85 and 1.5 (450 px tall: 0.85; 720: 1; 1080: 1.5). A label is at least
1 rem (13.6 px at 800×450); the display sizes are today's. The radar, the
gauge and the wall take the same scale; the wall's own steps (×1.2, ×1.4)
go.

**R4. The corners, finished.** Each still answers §17.2's one question.

- **The stars** (top right): five, outlined, no panel; the next star fills
  from its foot as the heat rises (no +n); the flash of R1.
- **The money block** under them: the bank first (the coin glyph, 22 px
  display, ink), the bag under it (the sack glyph, 44 px display, yellow,
  its × from ×1.3); the pops' lane under the block. The bank is first, so it
  never jumps and no gap is kept for the bag.
- **The combo** (top left): KOMBO, its points, its × from ×2 in ink, the
  last trick's word, its bar in ink.
- **The gauge** (bottom right) mirrors the radar: one round dial, 150 px at
  720p, on a dark disc like the radar's: the speed in its middle, km/h under it,
  the boost as its outer arc (270°, ink, a flame glyph at its start; full, it
  pulses once; boosting, it brightens), the damage as an inner arc for 3 s
  after a hit and standing from the third stage, in red. The words NITRO and
  USZKODZENIA go: the intro's SHIFT NITRO teaches the arc.
- The sack, the coin and the flame are outlines in `src/sim/glyphs.ts`, one
  drawing wherever they appear.

**R5. One moment, one message.**

- The top centre holds the goal line and at most one more: a caption, a
  card or a message. The rest waits, in this order: the intro's caption,
  the running job's card and its result, a warning (ROADBLOCK AHEAD,
  HELICOPTER), the stars' news, a teaching line, the chain's step card, a
  NEW card. News that has waited 4 s is dropped; cards wait.
- While a job's clock runs, the chain's step card and the NEW cards wait
  for its result.
- The busted moment: nothing speaks but the ticket. A police car alongside
  puts its prompt on the ticket as one block (E POŻYCZ); the disguise's
  clock shows once the car is borrowed.
- The key hints are four (drive, boost, map, pause) in one row for 8 s, in
  the first two sessions, only when the top is otherwise empty; the full
  list is on the pause screen.
- Inside the intro the captions speak alone; the stars' news and the
  district's name wait for a quiet moment. The damage shows on a hit, not
  standing (R4).
- **Teaching at the moment**, once per profile each, saved: the first bag
  (ŁUP · WPŁAĆ GO W GARAŻU), the first × (GWIAZDKI MNOŻĄ ŁUP), the first
  chase (ZGUB ICH · ZNIKNIJ IM Z OCZU).
- **A card** is a title, one line of at most 34 characters in either
  language, and its number; it stays 2.5 s at least. The line's clock and
  distance say the rest.

**R6. The radar answers three questions**: where to go, where the police
are, where to bank.

- It draws: the streets mid-grey on the dark ground, the highway and the
  loop as wider pale roads (no yellow); the district tints and the water;
  the route in cyan, 6 px on a dark 2 px edge each side, the brightest line
  of the disc; the goal's badge, 20 px (ink face, cyan ring, pictogram);
  open rings as 6 px ink dots and closed ones grey; the units blue (flashing
  with the stars while they chase), those on the beat grey, the search
  disc; a race's rivals as small red arrows; the nearest garage's house on
  the rim, only while the bag holds money or the line says BANK IT or BUY;
  a cache as a gold dot only within 80 m; the player as a 14 px ink arrow
  with a dark edge; N.
- The landmarks, the other garages, the caches beyond 80 m, the cameras,
  the cover and the breakers are on the full map only.
- 165 px at 720p, in the scale.
- **The full map** uses the same colours and marks, badges of 12 px with
  their pictograms, the route at 6 px, the player's ink arrow; its key lists
  only the kinds on the map now; each district's name sits at a point clear
  of every icon.

**R7. Signs that read and never block.**

- A sign is never smaller on screen than 28 px at 720p (in the scale):
  beyond the distance where its 1.4 m face would be, it is drawn larger
  about its centre. The goal's sign is 1.25× the others.
- A sign taller on screen than 15 % of the height folds away over 0.15 s
  and comes back when it is smaller or behind the camera: the camera never
  enters a sign.
- The label over the nearest open sign ahead within 100 m names its kind
  and its pay (UCIECZKA +3000), placed over the sign.
- No coins round the rings (§3.2's ring of seven); a running job's route
  coins stay.

**R8. The golden hour.** Starting values, tuned on the stills against §1.2:

- The sun about 25° over the horizon (51° today), keeping its bearing; warm
  gold (`#ffc27a`), about 2.4. The fill about half of today's (0.7): a cool
  violet sky (`#8f8fd6`) over a warm ground bounce (`#9a7560`). Shadows at
  0.9. Sunlit faces glow warm, shaded faces go violet.
- The sky in three stops, as at dusk: hot peach at the horizon (`#ffb487`),
  rose about 5° up (`#d9849a`), deep violet from about 15° up (`#3d3a7a`),
  so the frame's top 15 % (about 13°–22° up), where the HUD sits, is dark
  enough for ink at 4.5:1 without its outline. The fog and the background
  are the horizon's stop.
- `CITY_COLORS` a quarter more saturated, the trims and chalk kept light;
  each district's accent a clean, strong colour on its canopies, rails and
  fascias (Crown gold, the Works teal, the Gardens' flower boxes pink, the
  Quay aqua and coral); the asphalt a notch bluer; the markings brighter;
  the grass, the dirt and the sea under the same light.

**R9. The city lit at dusk, with depth.** A glow per vertex in the city's

camera as the dome does, one draw each.

**R13. The atmosphere** (Marcin, 2026-09-28). Seen on the stills that day:
the sun was a sticker (a flat white disc in a pink ring with an edge, its
halo lit behind a block as in the open, nothing ever burning: without tone
mapping the bright ends cut to white); the clouds were twelve alike flat
ovals in the sun's half, still; the sky was three stops a vertex, banded,
with nothing of an evening on the side away from the sun; nothing reflected
the sky (the sea one colour, the glass navy, the asphalt dark toward the
sun). One idea carries the fix: **the sky is one function, and everything
that shows the sky takes it from there.** No texture, no full-screen pass.

- **The sky per pixel.** One GLSL function of a direction, `skyAt`, from
  the `SKY` table: the three stops on smooth curves; toward the sun a wide
  glow over the haze and a tight one round the disc; away from it the
  evening's arch, a cool slate band at the horizon under a rose band a few
  degrees up (the Belt of Venus as seen a moment before sunset), all under
  12° so the HUD's band stays dark. The dome draws it with the sun's disc (a
  hot core, a darker limb) and a dither of half a level against banding; the
  fog takes the same function without the disc, so the far city dissolves
  into the sky's colour where it stands. The disc's own mesh goes.
- **Light with headroom.** The renderer tone maps with Khronos PBR
  Neutral's shoulder, in each material's shader, not in a pass: under 0.8
  it keeps the palette as it is, above it rolls the light off into warm
  white instead of cutting it. Neutral's toe stays out (slice 17: it took
  up to 0.04 off every channel and crushed the asphalt to black violet). The sun then shines stronger and warmer, the fill turns
  cooler, and the shade is violet, not night. The dome, the clouds and every
  shader that draws the world take the same curve, so the fogged city meets
  the sky without a seam.
- **Clouds with a body.** Low-poly clouds in the world's style: each a few
  flattened icosahedra, flat-shaded, lit per face from the sun and `skyAt`.
  Toward the sun their bodies are dark with a burning edge (the light
  through their thin rims); away from it their faces are rose and gold from
  below, their tops the sky's violet. Banks low round the whole horizon (3°
  to 12°, thicker in the sun's half) and a few long streaks 12°–20° up in
  the sun's half. They swing slowly to and fro about the vertical (12° each
  way over twenty minutes; a full turn would carry the thick banks away from
  the sun) and hide the sun as they pass it. One draw.
- **The world reflects the sky.** The sea in facets: its plane stays, a
  triangle grid's tilting normals are worked out per pixel, each facet the
  sky's colour where it reflects, by Fresnel, or the water's own; the facets
  that catch the sun flash, a path of light on the water toward it. The
  buildings' glass (a third byte in `cityLook`) and the cars' glass take
  the sky by Fresnel and a glint on the faces turned to the sun; the asphalt
  a broad warm sheen toward the sun at grazing angles.
- **The sun's glare.** A soft warm glow over everything round the sun's
  place on screen, drawn last (a quad, not a full-screen pass). Its strength
  is how directly the camera faces the sun times how much of the disc is
  seen: one occlusion query a frame on one of five points of the disc in
  turn, the last five answers averaged, so a block, a bus or a cloud over
  the sun puts the glare out within 0.15 s, and driving out from behind a
  block the sun blinds. No lens-flare rings, no bloom.

browser's rhythm with 44 ms hitches): a calm drive is already smooth (the
car wobbles 0.1–0.25 px on the screen, the horizon 0.1–0.35 px; the
hitches change nothing; a building hides the car 0.1 % of the time); the
rough parts are moments. The reverse view is an orbit added on top of the
chase's heading and started by the sign of the speed: a car turned 180° by
a ram turns the view a full 360° (peaks of 387°/s); a car pushed backwards
at 72 km/h is watched against its travel for 1.3 s; a back-up of 1 s off a
wall swings the view round and back (301°); its timer is not cleared at a
stop, so the next reverse swings at once and 1.2 s standing swings it back;
the swing peaks at 230–260°/s, twice the chase's own 110°/s. The
mega-ramp's fall leaves the camera 12 m over the car at the landing, which
then drops 8 m in 0.4 s and throws the car 50 px on the screen. A post by
the runway pulled the camera from 9.2 to 2.0 m in one frame. The jolt of a
hit or a landing is a sine on the world's axes: on an east–west road its
sideways half is gone, and it has no direction. On a downhill the road
35 m ahead sits 28 px lower on the screen than on the flat. BUSTED, the
wreck's 3 s, the mega-ramp's apex and the game's first second have no
shot.

How other games do it (research the same day: open-world games, arcade
racers, the literature and the engines' cameras). Most never turn the
camera round by themselves (GTA, Mafia, Cyberpunk, Watch Dogs, Forza
Horizon, NFS, The Crew, Rocket League, Mario Kart): it returns behind the
car's nose, and a held button looks back (B in CrazyGames' driving
games). A minority turn it (Sleeping Dogs, Midtown Madness 2, Carmageddon,
Wreckfest, BeamNG, iRacing), mostly as an option or a camera of its own,
and their players complain of what ours does: the whip, the slow way back,
the dizziness of quick forward and back, the whirl in a spin. The practice
(Haigh-Hutchinson's real-time cameras, Unity's car camera, Realistic Car
Controller, iRacing): the reverse gear decides, not the way the car moves;
one target and one smoother, never an orbit stacked on the chase; in a
spin the camera stops chasing the nose and follows the travel; the turn
capped and eased, the short way round, never back and forth in quick
succession.

- **One camera, one direction.** The view's yaw is one number with one
  smoother: a critically damped spring, its turn capped at 150°/s and eased
  in and out, the same at 30, 60 and 120 Hz; small errors follow as the
  chase did. The reverse view (the car's heading turned half round) only in
  the reverse gear, after 1.2 s of backing up: a back-up off a wall and a
  three-point turn leave the camera behind the car, a long reverse turns it
  once, and the gas brings it back at once. A slide tail first (a spin, a
  ram) is followed along its travel; slowed down, the view holds until the
  player chooses: the gas turns it behind the nose, the reverse keeps it.
- **The jolt comes from the physics.** A spring in the camera's own axes,
  a few tenths of a degree, kicked by the sim: a hit along its contact's
  normal as the camera sees it and by its speed change, a landing downward
  by its speed, a scrape as a fine tremble. The same hit reads the same in
  every direction of travel.
- **Big air.** In the air the camera never hangs more than 4 m over the
  car; on a long flight the look leans to where the car will land; the
  mega-ramp's apex gets a side shot, as a takedown does, and the chase is
  back before the landing.
- **The ground.** The view pitches with the road; only a wall, a roof or
  the slope pulls the camera in, never a post, a sign or a trunk; the
  camera never under 1.5 m over the ground under it.
- **The still moments.** BUSTED: while the ticket is up, the camera rises
  and draws back slowly over the units round the car. The wreck: it circles
  the wreck for the respawn's 3 s. The chase returns with control.
- **The first second.** The game opens on the island seen from above the
  summit (the sea, the sun) and comes down behind the car in 2 s; the
  player drives from the first frame, and the gas shortens the rest. The
  frame it opens on is the cover's (M9).
- **Under them**: each shot a pure function of the game's state (an eye
  and a look, pinned as the showroom's is), the director cutting to it or
  handing back to the chase with an eased blend.

### 1.4 What stays out, and why

- **The name, the logo, the tab's title and the cover** (Marcin: when the
  time comes). M9's cover brief takes this milestone's frames.
- **Textures, normal maps, a colour grade or a bloom pass.** The brief's
  flat-shaded, textureless look stays, and the low tier gets no new
  full-screen pass (the speed lines' stays the only one). The look comes
  from the light, the palette and the vertex colours. Tone mapping is not a
  pass: three runs it at the end of each material's shader (R13, Marcin
  2026-09-28); the sky's, the sea's and the glass's light are functions in
  the shaders, not textures.
- **Real lights in the city and a day–night cycle.** Lit windows and lamps
  are colour; the time of day is fixed (STYLE).
- **New systems, jobs, cars, HUD elements or balance numbers.** The rings'
  coins are the one change, quoted at the gate.
- **A second typeface** and any font service: the files are in the build.
- **Touch** (M9). M9 lays its buttons over the corners and moves the radar
  as its plan says; the scale and the gauge serve it.
- **Music and sound**, but for the horn heard on its card.
- **Rebuilding the city's geometry**: only the colours and the attributes
  of the meshes that are there.
- **A look-back key, a bonnet camera, roll in the corners, a camera turned
  with the mouse** (R14): where the police are is the radar's answer; the
  signs and the goal line are drawn for a camera 2 m up; roll makes players
  sick; the keyboard comes first.

## 7. API facts and traps

- **The scale is rem, not `zoom`**: CSS `zoom` multiplies viewport units
  inside the zoomed layer (a `100vh` wall grows past the screen) and moves
  every client rectangle. With rem, `getBoundingClientRect`, the full map's
  picking and the e2e overlap rule stay as they are. The media queries stay
  in px; the dev panel keeps px.
- **The pay label is placed in screen pixels** from the camera's projection:
  not in rem, and on the top layer, under the pause.
- **The canvases** (the radar, the full map, the gauge's arcs if drawn on
  one) size their backing store from their rect × DPR on every resize, and
  draw words only after `document.fonts.load('900 16px Rubik')`.
- **`font-display: swap`**: the first frames may use the fallback; the e2e
  waits for `document.fonts.ready` before it measures.
- **Rubik has no ★**: draw the star inline. Check its `tnum`; without
  tabular figures the speed and the money sit in fixed-width digit boxes.
- **An outline**: a ring of text-shadows works everywhere;
  `-webkit-text-stroke` eats into the glyph unless `paint-order: stroke
  fill` is honoured on HTML text. Check Chrome, Edge and Safari before
  choosing.
- **Never change the light count after boot**: three.js recompiles every lit
  program when lights are added, removed or hidden (a hitch at the door). The
  room's light exists from boot at intensity 0.
- **The showroom's framing** is `camera.setViewOffset`; clear it on the
  drive-out and set it again on a resize.
- **The turntable is drawn only**: `PlayerCar` adds a yaw while the door is
  shut; the sim's body never moves (the door and the drive-out read it).
- **The city's material chains `onBeforeCompile`**: `fadeShadowEdges` sets
  it, `roadPaint` wraps it; the glow wraps again and extends
  `customProgramCacheKey`. It goes after the lights and before
  `#include <fog_fragment>`, so the lit windows fade with distance.
- **The sky dome is 24×12 segments**: three stops fit its rings; the sun's
  disc is its own mesh. (Slice 4 made it 48×36; slice 16 draws the sky per
  pixel and the disc in the dome.)
- **The sky's function lives twice**: in GLSL for the dome and the fog, in
  TypeScript for the pins. One `SKY` table feeds both and one GLSL string is
  spliced into both shaders; the gate's look suite measures the real frame.
- **The fog chunks are patched once, before any program compiles**
  (`skyFog`, the third bug hunt): a change to `skyAt` after boot needs the
  programs rebuilt, so it is fixed at boot like the palette.
- **Tone mapping on a `ShaderMaterial`** needs `#include
  <tonemapping_fragment>` and `#include <colorspace_fragment>` at the end of
  its fragment, or the sky stays untouched while the fogged city beside it is
  mapped: a seam at the horizon. The speed lines are a screen effect and stay
  outside the curve. Three maps only what is drawn to the screen: the
  garage's pictures, drawn to a target, keep their studio light unmapped.
- **The clouds write depth and clear it after their draw**
  (`onAfterRender` → `renderer.clearDepth()`, three keeps its depth mask in
  its state cache): they hide each other, and the world drawn after them is
  never behind them, though a landmark 1 km off is farther than their 700 m.
  The glare's probe (slice 20) therefore cannot see them in the depth
  buffer; it tests the sun's five points against their puffs on the CPU.
- **An occlusion query's result is late**: WebGL2's
  `ANY_SAMPLES_PASSED_CONSERVATIVE` answers one to three frames on; read it
  only once `QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE`, never block on it, keep one in flight
  a point. The query is begun and ended round the probe's draw from its
  `onBeforeRender`/`onAfterRender` on `renderer.getContext()`; three's state
  cache is not touched.
- **`SUN_OFFSET` drives the light and the shadow's texel grid**
  (`shadows.ts`). A lower sun throws shadows past the map's 140 m, where
  `fadeShadowEdges` fades them; check acne at the grazing angle
  (`normalBias` 0.18).
- **The pictures' render target** takes `colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace` or the
  pictures come out dark; `readRenderTargetPixels` stalls the GPU, so a few
  cells a frame and only while the wall is up; flip the rows when copying.
- **The rings' coins** are `Coins.addExtra(…, 'rings')`, laid once by
  `jobs/place.ts`; removing them frees 256 slots and changes the jobs' coin
  counts.
- **The save's next version** after M8.8's carries the taught flags and the
  session count; the migration keeps every field.
- **`e2e/screens.spec.ts`** asserts `drivingShown`'s lists and English texts
  (TOTALS, CARS, BORROW); update them in the slice that changes each, and
  run the suite at the gate.
- **M8.8's bodies and kit need cells**; its role and BEST AT lines stay on
  the cards.
- **No per-frame DOM writes**: the outline makes a text's repaint dearer;
  the voice and the corners write on change only, as now.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`**: the stars steady red, the turntable still,
  the goal's sign without its bob.
